# Advice System

An advice marks part of an instrument's scale: a range or a single value the operator should steer into, or one to keep out of. Every instrument takes the same list and draws it with the same shapes and colours, so an optimal range looks alike on a compass, a bar and a chart.

<Canvas of={AdviceStories.AdviceComparison} sourceState="none" />

## Quick start

Give the instrument a list. Each entry names its category and a range, or a single value for a point:

```js
const gauge = document.querySelector('obc-gauge-radial');
gauge.advices = [
  {min: 40, max: 70, category: 'optimal'},
  {min: 85, max: 100, category: 'caution'},
  {value: 95, category: 'alarm'},
];
```

The instrument does the rest: a band turns triggered while the instrument's value or setpoint is inside it.

In a Lit template the list is a property binding:

```js
html`<obc-gauge-radial
  minValue="0"
  maxValue="100"
  .value=${55}
  .advices=${advices}
></obc-gauge-radial>`;
```

## When to use

- **Show advices while they apply.** An advice comes from a decision-support, assistance or planning mode. Show the list while that mode is on and clear it when the mode ends. The vue demo, for one, shows its advices only while the A key is held.
- **Keep permanent limits on the scale.** A limit that is always there belongs to the scale's tick marks and labels.
- **Pick the category by intent.** A go-to category marks a value to follow, a no-go category a value to avoid.

## Categories

The alert categories take their alert colours when triggered, so an alarm limit reads as the alarm it is.

<Markdown>{categories}</Markdown>

Lists written for earlier releases keep working: `advice` is read as `optimal` and `safe` as `running`. `obc-readout-block` takes the same category names. An `ai` category for simulated or analysed suggestions waits for its colour token.

## States

Every category has four looks. The list sets the state a band rests in, and the instrument switches a band to `triggered` while a reading is inside it.

<Markdown>{states}</Markdown>

### Emphasis

`adviceEmphasis` chooses the resting states for you, the way the design describes. With nothing triggered, go-to bands are `active` and no-go bands `enabled`. When a go-to band triggers, the no-go bands fade to `hinted`, and the other way round. It is off by default, because how strongly an advice should stand out is part of the application's alert philosophy.

## Shapes

- **Range** (`min`, `max`): a band beside the bar on the tick side, or on the ring. A go-to range is a pill, a no-go range is square with a small corner, and two bands that meet are cut flat where they join.
- **Point** (`value`): a go-to point is a dot, a no-go point is a short thick tick, the way a limit is drawn.
- **Area** (`showArea: true` on a range or a point): limit lines across the bar, the face or the chart, dotted for go-to and dashed or solid for no-go, tinted while triggered.

<Canvas of={AdviceStories.AdviceRangeAndPoint} sourceState="none" />

A tick mark at a band's end cuts a no-go band flush and insets a go-to pill. `hideMinTickmark` and `hideMaxTickmark` leave that end rounded instead.

## API

### Properties

Every instrument that draws advices has these three properties, with two exceptions listed under [Instruments](#instruments): `obc-azimuth-thruster` has two lists, `angleAdvices` and `thrustAdvices`, that share one trigger and emphasis, and `obc-pitch-roll-heave` has `heaveAdvice` alone. `obc-gauge-trend`, `obc-automation-tank` and `obc-depth` also keep the whole layer behind `hasAdvice`, which is off by default.

<Markdown>{properties}</Markdown>

### Advice entries

<Markdown>{adviceEntry}</Markdown>

The types come from `@oicl/openbridge-webcomponents/dist/svghelpers/advice.js`: `AdviceCategory`, `AdviceState`, `AdviceTrigger`, `AdviceInput`, and `ResolvedAdvice` for an entry after the instrument has resolved it.

### Angular ranges

On a compass or any other angular instrument, a range runs clockwise from `min` to `max` and may cross north: `{min: 350, max: 10}` is a 20° band. A pair written the other way round, whose clockwise span would exceed 180°, is drawn the short way, so `{min: 200, max: 100}` is the band from 100 to 200. Write a band longer than 180° with the smaller angle first.

## Areas on a chart

`obc-line-graph`, `obc-area-graph` and `obc-gauge-trend` draw an entry with `showArea` on the chart: the tint between the limits under the data, the limit lines over it. `obc-gauge-trend` also draws the bands on its side bar and triggers them on its value and setpoint, and shows neither until `hasAdvice` is on. A plain line graph has no reading and no such gate, so its bands keep the state the list gives them.
